### Executive Summary

If senior Tatmadaw leadership, particularly Min Aung Hlaing, continues to face no meaningful international legal accountability (ICC, targeted sanctions enforcement, or diplomatic isolation), Myanmar is most likely to enter a **prolonged period of fragmented, high-cost civil conflict** characterized by volatile equilibrium and institutional bifurcation.

The absence of accountability reinforces the regime’s sense of impunity, sustains the **Duality Trap**, and reduces incentives for genuine political compromise. All current indicators — continued fighting across multiple fronts, persistent rhetoric-operation divergence, and fragmented resistance structures — point toward **extended multi-sided warfare** rather than decisive resolution or national reconciliation.

**Base Case Outlook (60–70% probability):** Grinding stalemate with periodic escalations through at least 2028. No side achieves national dominance. Humanitarian suffering remains severe.

**Humanitarian Buffer Zones:** Limited, localized corridors are possible near borders, but large-scale third-party military buffers between fighting forces remain highly improbable.

---

### 1. Strategic Context (May 2026)

The 2026 quasi-presidential transition has created a civilian-facing “Mask” while preserving the military’s “Iron Line.” However, active armed conflict continues in multiple regions. The Tatmadaw retains superiority in airpower and heavy weapons but faces chronic recruitment difficulties and stretched logistics. Resistance forces have gained significant territory but remain politically and militarily fragmented.

Regional powers prioritize border stability and economic interests over transformative justice. Global attention is diluted by competing crises.

---

### 2. Consequences of an Accountability Vacuum

**Short-term (2026–2027)**
- Reinforced regime confidence in the survival strategy, leading to intensified **Compensation Effect** (soft rhetoric in Naypyidaw matched by escalated operations in the periphery).
- Sustained internal military cohesion through doctrinal reaffirmation (“Military as Parents”).
- Continued fragmentation of resistance efforts due to lack of unified command and major external backing.
- Accelerated humanitarian deterioration: displacement, economic collapse, expansion of criminal economies (scams, narcotics).

**Medium-to-Long Term (2028–2030)**
- Further de facto territorial balkanization and emergence of ethnic strongholds.
- Deepened societal trauma and radicalization of new generations.
- Progressive erosion of central state institutions and governance capacity.
- Increased regional spillover (refugees, transnational crime, border tensions).

### 4. Critical Indicators to Monitor (SSISM Early Warning Metrics)

- Intensity of **Compensation Effect** (rhetoric vs. kinetic activity gap)
- Tatmadaw air sortie rates, recruitment success, and desertion levels
- Degree of political-military coordination among resistance forces
- Chinese and Indian diplomatic posture shifts
- Trends in international sanctions enforcement and ICC momentum
- Humanitarian access metrics and new displacement waves

---

### Key Takeaway for Public Intelligence

The absence of accountability for past and ongoing crimes does not produce stability — it entrenches a **self-reinforcing cycle of violence and institutional fragility**. Myanmar faces years of painful, fragmented conflict rather than clean resolution.

Strategic awareness, disciplined documentation, improved resistance coordination, and sustained international evidence preservation remain the most rational responses in this environment.

Surface-level diplomatic “normalization” should not be mistaken for sustainable peace. The **Duality Trap** remains unresolved.
