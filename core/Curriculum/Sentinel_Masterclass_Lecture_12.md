🌿 SSISM Intel Sentinel Masterclass Lecture 12

"When 'DUM!' Is Too Late"

A Mobile Game, the Buddha's Wisdom, and the Weight of Human Decisions

Bamar Enlightenment Journal 34


---

Opening Question

A young player asks:

> "In the game, if I make a mistake, I hear 'DUM!' and I simply press Try Again."



The teacher replies:

> "Son, that is the mercy of a game.

Life does not always have a 'Try Again' button."




---

The Buddha's First Lesson

The Buddha repeatedly taught that intentional actions (kamma) have consequences.

A familiar Pali verse is:

> "Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā."



"Mind precedes all phenomena."

Before the hand moves,

the mind moves.

Before the mouth speaks,

the mind chooses.

Before the feet walk,

the intention has already taken a direction.


---

The Four Directions

The game asks:

Forward?

Backward?

Left?

Right?


The Buddha asks something deeper:

"What intention is moving your mind?"

The direction of the body begins with the direction of the mind.


---

The Sound of "DUM!"

In the game,

"DUM!"

means:

> Try again.



In reality,

sometimes the consequence comes immediately.

Examples include:

One reckless moment can cause a traffic accident.

One dishonest act can break trust.

One hateful message can deeply hurt another person.


Not every mistake can be undone.

This is why wisdom before action matters.


---

Avijjā and Vijjā

Avijjā (Ignorance):

"I'll think later."

Vijjā (Knowledge):

"I'll think first."

Sometimes the greatest victory is not making the move at all until you understand it.


---

The Buddha's Advice

The Buddha encouraged careful reflection before acting.

A practical question is:

> "Will this action lead to harm for myself or others?"



If yes,

pause.

If no,

continue carefully.


---

Three Questions for Young Leaders

Before every important decision:

1. Is it true?


2. Is it beneficial?


3. Is it the right time?



If the answer is uncertain,

waiting may be wiser than rushing.


---

Final Reflection

The game teaches courage.

Life teaches responsibility.

The game gives another character.

Life asks us to care for the one we already have.


---

Closing Verse

> Games teach skill.

Life teaches responsibility.

Wisdom is learning the difference before hearing the final "DUM!"
