# Lecture 01: What Is Intelligence?

## Teaching Goal

This lecture explains intelligence as a civil, ethical, and disciplined capacity of the mind.  
It is designed for activists, civil society workers, and young leaders.

## English Definition

Intelligence is the disciplined capacity of the mind to perceive reality accurately, analyze it critically, and act wisely with self-control and ethical responsibility.

Intelligence includes:
- awareness,
- understanding,
- judgment,
- and responsible action.

## Why It Matters

For civil society, intelligence is not hidden spying.  
It is open, careful, and verifiable understanding.

True intelligence helps people:
- reduce mistakes,
- control emotion,
- verify information,
- and act with discipline.

## SSISM Meaning

In SSISM, intelligence means a sovereign and open process of:
- seeing what is happening,
- checking whether it is true,
- thinking in context,
- and acting responsibly.

This is civil intelligence, not secret manipulation.

## Key Contrast

Spy-style intelligence:
- hidden,
- fearful,
- secretive,
- controlled from above.

SSISM civil intelligence:
- open,
- verifiable,
- ethical,
- people-centered.

## Teaching Sentence

**Intelligence is the disciplined use of mind to see truth, verify reality, and act without error.**

## Discussion Questions

1. What does intelligence mean in daily civic life?
2. Why is open verification important?
3. How is intelligence different from spying?
4. How can young leaders use intelligence to avoid mistakes?

## Short Practice

Ask learners to take one news item and do four steps:
1. See.
2. Check.
3. Think.
4. Act.
