# Lecture 03: Civil Intelligence Workflow

## Teaching Goal

This lecture teaches a practical workflow for civil intelligence in open society work.  
It helps activists, civil society workers, and young leaders turn information into disciplined action.

## Main Idea

Civil intelligence is not guessing.  
It is a method.

The method is:
**See → Check → Think → Act**

## Step 1: See

Seeing means observing what is happening in public life.

Examples:
- news headlines,
- speeches,
- official statements,
- community reports,
- public documents,
- and social signals.

## Step 2: Check

Checking means verifying whether the information is true.

Questions to ask:
- Who said this?
- What is the source?
- Is there evidence?
- Can it be confirmed elsewhere?
- Is the information current?

## Step 3: Think

Thinking means analyzing the meaning of the information.

Ask:
- What is the context?
- What is the bias?
- Who benefits?
- What are the risks?
- What are the possible consequences?

## Step 4: Act

Action means responding in a disciplined and ethical way.

Examples:
- archive the information,
- share verified facts,
- write a summary,
- alert the community,
- or prepare a public response.

## Why Workflow Matters

Without workflow:
- people react too quickly,
- rumor spreads easily,
- mistakes increase,
- and public trust weakens.

With workflow:
- facts become clearer,
- errors are reduced,
- discipline improves,
- and communities become stronger.

## Open-Source Practice

SSISM civil intelligence should be:
- transparent,
- repeatable,
- documented,
- and shareable.

This means the community can learn the method and use it again and again.

## Simple Example

A headline appears about a political issue.

1. See the headline.
2. Check the source and the date.
3. Think about the context and purpose.
4. Act by sharing only verified information.

## Teaching Sentence

**Civil intelligence is the disciplined process of transforming public information into verified understanding and responsible action.**

## Discussion Questions

1. Why should we not react before checking?
2. How does analysis improve action?
3. What happens when rumor replaces verification?
4. How can civil society use this workflow every day?
