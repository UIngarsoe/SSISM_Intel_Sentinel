Abstract

Bamar Enlightenment Journal 26 introduces the concept of Civic Intelligence as the collective capacity of communities to recognize, understand, and solve shared social challenges. Building upon the cognitive defense architecture established in BEJ 25, this lecture expands the discussion from individual intelligence toward collective intelligence, community awareness networks, civic participation, and public problem-solving systems.

Using Douglas Schuler's Civic Intelligence framework, international civic education models, and the Australian Neighbourhood Watch experience observed during more than three decades of residence in Melbourne, this journal demonstrates how ordinary citizens can become Community Intelligence Nodes that strengthen social resilience without relying solely on formal institutions.

The central lesson of this lecture is simple:

«Civic Intelligence begins when many minds cooperate.»

Through knowledge, attitude, skills, engagement, and participation, communities can build resilient civic ecosystems capable of responding to misinformation, fraud, social disruption, and local challenges through collective awareness and coordinated action.
