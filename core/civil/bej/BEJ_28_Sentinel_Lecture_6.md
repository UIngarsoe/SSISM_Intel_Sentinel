## 2. Case Study 5: The “Banana Diplomacy” Macroeconomic Fallacy
### The Narrative (ဇာတ်ကြောင်း)
The administrative apparatus announces the successful signing of numerous multilateral trade frameworks (e.g., 18 trade agreements with a neighboring power to export primary agricultural assets like bananas and marine resources), framing the development as a masterstroke of economic acumen, resource mobilization, and national growth.
### The Operational Reality (လက်တွေ့အရှိတရား)
This dynamic highlights structural economic vulnerability and asymmetric trade concessions:
 * **Asymmetric Tariff Influx:** Granting zero-tariff access to foreign manufactured industrial goods systematically destabilizes remaining local production pipelines and domestic small enterprises.
 * **Tax Shifting:** To compensate for the severe loss of state customs and tariff revenues, the administration shifts the fiscal burden onto the domestic population through aggressive internal taxation and localized levies.
 * **Primary Commodity Dependency:** Exporting low-value, raw agricultural materials (bananas) while importing high-value technological, industrial, or logistics assets creates long-term macroeconomic bleeding.
